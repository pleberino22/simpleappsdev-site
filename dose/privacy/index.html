Privacy

What you take is nobody else’s business. Dose is built so that it can’t become anyone’s.

Nothing leaves your iPhone

Dose makes no network requests. Not for sync, not for backup, not for analytics, not for anything — the app has no server to talk to and no account to talk about. The only connection involved anywhere is Apple’s own, when you buy the unlock.

That means your stacks, your schedule, what you take and when you took it never travel. They sit in Dose’s own database on the device and stay there.

There is no account

No sign-up, no login, no email address, no profile. Dose never asks who you are, and nothing it stores could be tied back to you if it did leave — which it doesn’t.

What Dose keeps, and for how long

Your stacks and inventory stay until you change or delete them. The record of doses you’ve taken is kept for 60 days and then removed automatically — Dose only needs recent history to show you today, so it doesn’t hoard the rest.

Notifications are the only permission

Dose asks for one thing: permission to send notifications, and only at the moment it first needs them. It uses them for two purposes — reminding you a stack is due, and telling you an item is running low. Both are scheduled by your iPhone, on your iPhone; nothing is sent from anywhere else and none of them report back.

A single switch in Settings turns every reminder off and removes the pending ones. Dose asks for no camera, no photos, no contacts, no location and no health data.

Purchases

The 7-day trial and the one-time unlock are handled entirely by Apple’s In-App Purchase system. Payment happens in Apple’s sheet, not in Dose, and I never see your card, your billing details or your Apple ID. Dose only learns whether the unlock has been purchased.

No analytics, no tracking, no ads

There is no analytics package, no advertising and no tracking of any kind, and no third-party code in the app at all. Dose never asks to track you across other apps, because it has nothing to track you with.

Deleting your data

Delete the app and everything it stored goes with it. There is no backup on my side, no account to close and no deletion request to file. Inside the app, stacks and inventory items can each be deleted individually.
